The story

The first thing he ever did about his Door was walk past it.

Harrow’s Shelf is a town cut into the side of a mountain and strung together with rope. Everything gets where it is going on somebody’s back. Keel is the somebody.

Then a Door appears in the sky over the Span. Plain planks, iron hinges, a handle worn bright, standing on empty air and facing exactly one person. Nobody sees your Door. But everybody knows the look of someone seeing one. Keel does what he has always done: finishes the delivery, shores up the whole town for three days, and lets nobody ask him a single question about himself.

Then he goes down. The Toll-stair takes its price on the way, and the price is memory. Names dissolve as he thinks them. He arrives at the bottom with nothing: no name, no class, no plan, and a Voice in the dark that counts everything.

Below are the floors, and every floor has a Guardian, and a Guardian is never killed, only relieved of what it carries. Every drop is an ordinary object, a bowl, a cord, a hat, heavy with a truth that was in the room when it happened. At the bottom stands a three-sided stone with one face waiting to be earned. And past it, a Wall built out of everything Keel never said.

From Chapter 1 · The Door

The opening.

I have carried a lot of things across the Span. This one felt like it broke me.

Not the load. The load was nothing, forty pounds of stove iron for the widow Corl, strapped high and tight the way I like it, riding my shoulders like it had somewhere better to be. I’ve hauled triple that up the Ladder Stairs in sleet, and I was seventeen years old and stupid about it, the way you’re stupid about the one thing you’re actually good at. No. What broke me was what I saw when I reached the middle of the highest bridge in Harrow’s Shelf, eleven hundred feet of empty morning air under my boots, and looked up to check the wind-flags.

There was a door hanging in the sky.

How the deep works

Nothing down there can be hurt until it is named.

A dungeon crawl with a system, and the system is honest. It doesn’t reward speed. It rewards the truth, said out loud, in front of someone.

01

The Door

It Calls exactly one person. A Door faces its Called, and nobody else sees so much as a shadow. Plain planks, iron hinges, a handle worn bright. No thunder, no light, no choir. You can walk past it. For a while.

02

The Toll

The stair down takes its price in memory. Names dissolve as you think them. You reach the bottom with nothing. And the Toll never stops collecting.

[WELCOME, TOLLED.]

03

The Voice

The deep keeps count, and it tells you. Names known. Times risen. Carried. It is cruel at first, then accurate, then something closer to company. It is not your friend. Keep reading.

04

The Unnamed

Weapons pass straight through a thing that hasn’t been named. Read the blur. Say what it truly is. Then fight what appears. A name someone tells you doesn’t count. Nobody can do this part for you.

05

The Guardians

One to a floor, each made of something people carry: swallowed fear, rehearsed fear, the dread of what’s next, the load nobody would set down. You don’t kill a Guardian. You relieve it.

06

The Artifacts

An ordinary thing that was present at an extraordinary truth. A bowl. A cord with twelve knots. A spoon carried from home and never dropped. They do nothing at all. They just get heavier.

The Guardians

Each one is a harder version of don’t say it.

They are not monsters. They are made of what people carry. The first one is knee-high and casts a shadow three stories tall.

  1. FlinchEveryday swallowed fear. Knee-high, faceless, and it casts a shadow three stories tall.
  2. DraggleA fear rehearsed until it learned to walk. Habits can’t improvise.
  3. Gholm, Who Waits BehindThe dread of what’s next. It only exists behind you. The entire advice is: do not turn around.
  4. Bracken, the Load That Is Never Set DownEvery load nobody would put down. You cannot beat a carrier. You can only relieve one.
  5. Selle, the Song That Doesn’t StopThe goodbyes the living never finished. Some Guardians are letters waiting for a reply.
  6. The AuditA whole town’s postponed reckonings, at raid scale. It is a desk. Its attacks are invoices.
  7. The WallEverything he never said. It cannot be beaten by force, because force is what it eats.

A Guardian relieved gives up an artifact. A Guardian merely killed gives up loot, and nothing banks. Precision has a body count.

Three sides · three books

At the bottom of every deep stands a three-sided stone.

Past the last Guardian there is a trestle, carved in a language nobody reads any more. Each face is blank until the deep’s Tolled earns the virtue that belongs to it. One face per book. It is the same shape as the Grit Triangle, because it is the Grit Triangle.
