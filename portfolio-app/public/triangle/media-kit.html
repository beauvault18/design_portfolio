About

The guy who always comes back

Beau Whitman grew up in a gym. He trained at Parkettes National Gymnastics Training Center, made the U.S. Men’s Junior Olympic National Team, pole vaulted for Lehigh University in NCAA Division I, and later competed in Olympic weightlifting. He also had seven reconstructive surgeries, and he trained his way back from every one.

In 2019, while coaching Division III track and field at NYU and teaching more than thirty fitness classes a week, an injury at a weightlifting meet sent him to the ER. The scan found cancer. He was told it was Stage 4 non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma. It turned out to be a rare Stage 3 nonseminomatous germ cell tumor. Chemotherapy, a ten-hour surgery, and one more reconstruction brought him to ten surgeries.

His toughness got him through treatment, but it didn’t heal him. Healing started the day he finally let his family see him break. That is the heart of everything he teaches now: being tough isn’t the same as being healed.

The framework

The Grit Triangle

Beau built the Grit Triangle in a chemo chair. It measures grit on three sides: vulnerability, resilience, and courage. Like any strong structure, it only stands when all three hold.

Every listener can take the free assessment at beauwhitman.org and find their own shape, so audiences leave with something they can use the same day.
